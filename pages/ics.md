# Calendar feeds

Subscribe once and deadlines appear in your calendar as they are added. These
URLs are permanent; the files behind them are rebuilt whenever the site is.

## The feeds

- [deadlines.ics](/ics/deadlines.ics) — submission, abstract and notification
  dates. The one most people want.
- [events.ics](/ics/events.ics) — when the conferences actually meet, for
  planning travel.
- [all.ics](/ics/all.ics) — both together.

Each covers everything upcoming plus the past year. Every conference also has
its own feed, linked from its page, covering all its recorded editions.

### By filter

Four feeds matching the filters on the front page. Same deadlines, fewer of them.

- `/ics/core-a.ics` — conferences ranked CORE A* or A in the year they met
- `/ics/open-access.ics` — proceedings free to read
- `/ics/europe.ics` — meeting in Europe
- `/ics/asia.ics` — meeting in Asia

The region feeds follow the edition, not the series: a conference that meets in
Lisbon one year and Kyōto the next appears in both.

## How to subscribe

Use *subscribe to calendar*, not *import*: importing copies the events once and
they never update again.

- **Apple Calendar** — File, New Calendar Subscription, paste the URL
- **Google Calendar** — Other calendars, +, From URL
- **Thunderbird** — New Calendar, On the Network, iCalendar (ICS)
- **Outlook** — Add calendar, Subscribe from web

Clients typically refresh a subscribed calendar every few hours to once a day.
The feeds ask for twelve hours.

## A word about times

Every entry is an **all-day event**, deliberately. A deadline is a moment, not a
day, but writing it as a moment would impose a time zone on you — and "23:59
Anywhere on Earth" rendered in your local time is more likely to mislead than to
help. The zone the organisers stated, or the fact that they stated none, is in
the event description.

So: treat the day shown as the day the deadline falls, and check the exact hour
on the conference's own page before relying on the last afternoon of it.

## When a deadline moves

The event is updated in place rather than replaced, so an extension moves the
entry already in your calendar instead of leaving a duplicate behind. That is
what the site is for.
