About ABZ
----------

The ABZ conference is dedicated to the cross-fertilization of state-based
and machine-based formal methods, like Abstract State Machines (ASM),
Alloy, B, TLA, VDM, and Z, that share a common conceptual foundation and
are widely used in academia and industry to design and analyze hardware and
software systems. The conference aims to exchange knowledge and experience
among the research communities around different formal methods.

The name ABZ refers to the first conference in London in 2008, where the
ASM, B, and Z conference series were merged into a joint
event. In the following years, other formal methods were added, e.g., Alloy
in 2010 (Orford, Canada), VDM in 2012 (Pisa, Italy), and
TLA + in 2014 (Toulouse, France). After the successful 2016 conferences in
Linz, Austria, and 2018 in Southampton, UK, it was
decided to name the conference "ABZ: International Conference on Rigorous
State Based Methods" to stress the openness for further
state-based formal methods. We hope to continue many fruitful discussions
between representatives of the individual methods in the
past, which will bring us closer to the common goal of this research
community: the creation of reliable and safe software.
ABZ2020 and ABZ2021 were planned in Ulm but virtually organized. With the
edition 2023 in Nancy, the ABZ conference returned to be a live event.

ABZ 2024 will have a main conference track, a case study track, a doctoral
symposium, tutorials, and workshops.


---------------
Main ABZ Track
---------------
Contributions are solicited on all aspects of the theory and applications
of ASMs, Alloy, B, TLA, VDM, Z, and other state-based rigor
approaches in software/hardware engineering, including developing tools and
industrial applications.
The program spans from theoretical and methodological foundations to
practical applications, emphasizing system engineering methods
and tools that are distinguished by mathematical rigor and have proved to
be industrially viable.
The main goal of the conference is to contribute to the integration of
accurate state- and machine-based system development methods,
clarifying their commonalities and differences to understand better how to
combine different approaches for accomplishing the various
tasks in modeling, experimental validation, and mathematical verification
of reliable, high-quality hardware/software systems.
Although organized to host several formal methods in a single event,
editorial control of the joint conference is vested in one
integrated program committee.


-----------------
Case Study Track
-----------------
As successfully practiced since ABZ 2014, the 10th edition of ABZ will
again include special sessions dedicated to an industrial case study.
We explicitly invite you to also submit contributions to case studies from
previous conferences, which substantially extend the solutions presented
in one aspect or another. Possible enhancements could be new proof
techniques, more elegant modeling, generation, verification, or validation
of executable code, etc.

See https://abz-conf.org/site/2024/casestudy/ for a detailed description of
the new case study and links to the previous ones.

Workshop and Tutorial Proposals
--------------------------------
Workshops and tutorials will be associated with the main event ABZ.
Proposals are solicited in areas related to the conference topics.

A workshop proposal should contain the workshop title and acronym, the
contact information for the workshop organizers, an abstract for the ABZ
2024 website (if the workshop is accepted), the description of the
workshop, and the expected format (organization, duration, expected
participants, etc.).

A tutorial proposal should contain the title of the tutorial, the contact
information of the tutorial presenter(s), the tutorial summary, and a brief
CV of the
tutorial presenters for the ABZ 2024 website (if accepted), and the
tutorial format (organization, duration, etc.).

Proposals can be sent to claudio.menghi@unibg.it and
alexander.raschke@uni-ulm.de


------------
Submissions
------------
Four kinds of contributions are invited:

*  Full research papers:
   Full research papers must be original, unpublished, and not submitted
elsewhere.
   A paper of up to 16 pages (excluding references) in LNCS format is
expected and will be reviewed.

*  Short papers:
   Short papers to present and validate a work in progress or tool
demonstrations. A paper of no more than 6 pages (excluding references) in
LNCS format
   is expected and will be reviewed.

*  Application in industry papers:
   Reporting on work or experiences on applying state-based formal methods
in industry. A paper of no more than 8 pages (excluding references)
   in LNCS format is expected and will be reviewed. It is also an exciting
option for industrial practitioners who sometimes need more time
   to prepare a full paper.

*  Journal-First papers:
   The journal-first paper summarizes recently published papers in
high-quality journals. Journal-first papers aim to enrich the program by
further
   ABZ and to provide more visibility to an already published journal paper
in the scope of the ABZ conference. An extended abstract of no more than 6
   pages (excluding references) in LNCS format is expected and will be
reviewed.

*  Case study papers:
   Full papers reporting on the experiments conducted with any of the
state-based techniques in the scope of the ABZ 2024 case study.
   A paper of up to 16 pages (excluding references) in LNCS format is
expected and will be reviewed.
   We also expect a link to a webpage to download the produced models.

*  Contribution to the doctoral symposium:
   This is an excellent opportunity for Ph.D. students to present their
ongoing work and receive valuable feedback from the ABZ community.
    A paper of at most 4 pages (excluding references) in LNCS format is
expected and will be reviewed.

Accepted papers will appear in the Springer LNCS proceedings. The deadlines
for the different kinds of contributions are above.
